How the evidence is graded

  • Direct A source shows the property changes how people understand an AI explanation, a probability or forecast, or a warning message.
  • Indirect A source shows the property affects comprehension in general text or interfaces, or in people with dementia, MCI or autism. Applying it to explanations is our inference.
  • None found No source links it to understanding. Treat it as general UI (A) or as our inference.

No source tests any of these guidelines on AI explanations for people with dementia, MCI, autism or ADHD. Even a "Direct" grade means the link to our population is our inference. Each source is labelled with how it was checked: full text, or abstract (publisher pages were blocked, so the abstract record was read). Nothing comes from a search snippet alone. "Counter" marks evidence against the guideline.

Grounding for the whole set: raw XAI output does not suit lay users

  • Miller (2019), Artificial Intelligence 267. It is "not reasonable to expect a lay-user to be able to interpret a causal chain"; explanations are social and fitted to what the listener knows. full text
  • Liao, Gruen & Miller (2020), CHI. Lay users ask for explanations; practitioners found LIME feature-importance lists "unsatisfying"; users ask "what do you mean by…" questions that current algorithms don't answer. full text
  • Schuff et al. (2022), FAccT. Lay users "often mis-interpret" feature-attribution explanations, including Shapley-based ones. full text
  • Tielman et al. (2024), Technology in Society 79. "Adaptive XAI is the key to inclusive XAI"; it asks for personalisation, "more than just simplicity in text". full text
